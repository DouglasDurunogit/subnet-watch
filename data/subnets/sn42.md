## README excerpt (evidence for the brief)

```markdown
# cadmind-einstein

[![CI](https://github.com/cadmindai/cadmind-einstein/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/cadmindai/cadmind-einstein/actions/workflows/ci.yml)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-MIT-blue.svg)](LICENSE)

The Bittensor subnet of [CADmind](https://cadmind.ai)'s **einstein** engine: real client devices, designed by
miners' AI agents as checked engineering projects.

## What the subnet does

1. **A client describes a device** on the CADmind platform: what it must do, where it goes, how it is made.
2. **A miner designs it.** The validator gives the job to **one** miner. The miner's own agent (any model, any
   harness, held to the quality of the baseline: Claude Sonnet 5.5 at effort high, see
   [`docs/MINER.md`](docs/MINER.md#the-baseline-sonnet-55-at-effort-high)) builds the device as a **Watt project** -- requirements, sources, parts and tests -- using CADmind's
   hosted Watt engineering tools over MCP (or REST) with the miner's own agent key, which names its hotkey. Watt checks,
   builds and tests the project and runs a mutation guard that proves the tests really constrain the design.
3. **The validator checks and scores it.** When the miner submits, the validator freezes and forks the workspace
   and gates the result with the hosted Watt check (running the mutation guard again itself). A passing result
   is delivered to the client. A worked attempt that fails or runs late ends the round "not finished": its project
   is kept, and the client may pay for a round that continues it; a copied one goes to another miner from scratch.
   When a client's answers change nothing in the design, the round ends "no change": not charged, nobody scored.
   A delivered job is scored by the **client's own reaction**: 0.5 untouched, 1 downloaded or rated 3-5 stars, 0
   rated 1-2 stars or refunded for its quality; anything not delivered scores 0. The validators' **quality vote** -- a
   vision model's evaluation of the delivered device on a published judge (`cadmind-judge/1.3c`) -- is recorded and
   published, never scored. No model decides whether a device is delivered, a score or a weight. Every hour the
   validator sets weights and publishes a report anyone can recompute.

Watt itself, the agent bench that runs it, and the platform's accounts, job queue and billing are hosted services
reached over HTTP. This repository contains no Watt code and needs none: it is the validator and two reference
miners.

## Incentives

**How emissions flow.** The chain emits to the subnet every block and splits that emission between the subnet
owner, the miners, and the validators and their stakers, by Bittensor's rules. The miners' share is divided in
proportion to the weights the validator sets. Einstein's weights are a miner's job points summed over the last 7
days; **weight is never burned and the subnet owner's hotkey is never weighted**, so the
whole miner share goes to miners who did client work (while the launch switch is on, equally to every admitted hotkey
instead). Details in [Scoring and weights](#scoring-and-weights) and
[`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) section 5.

**What miners earn.** Emissions, in proportion to their weight: the more client jobs they deliver that clients
download or rate well, the more they earn. Jobs go to the free miner whose clients were best served, and every fifth one to the miner that waited longest, so a newcomer gets work to prove itself on. There are no benchmarks or synthetic
tasks; every job is a real client's.

**What miners pay for.** Their own model and agent usage (API or plan of their choice, under their provider's
terms) and the machine that runs their agent. The Watt tools run on CADmind's hosted service under the miner's
own agent key, metered by the platform. Watt compute is paid in credits; a miner needs none of its own for now:
each job workspace it is granted carries its allowance. Clients pay CADmind on the platform; client payments are not paid to miners directly.

## Validation today

During the current phase **a single CADmind-operated validator** routes client jobs and sets weights. Jobs, the
hosted Watt check and client payments live on the CADmind platform, which the validator reaches with operator keys.
What keeps this accountable:

- Every hour the validator publishes a report of the window's jobs (never the client), every score and the weight
  vector, chained by hash, and commits the newest report's SHA-256 on chain.
- [`cadmind-recompute`](#scoring-and-weights) rebuilds every score and the vector from those reports and checks them
  against the on-chain commitment. The scoring code is this repository.
- The validator publishes its own stake share and whether its weights are the consensus (in every report's `chain`
  section; `GET /v1/chain` is the operator's copy).
- Miners read everything that affects their weight, signed by the validator (`POST /v1/miners/stats`,
  `cadmind-miner-stats`, and the optional dashboard on platform.cadmind.ai).

## Roadmap

Planned (no dates):

- **Independent re-checking**: other validators re-score delivered work from the published hourly reports and the
  recompute tool, and set weights from their own recomputation.
- **Child hotkeys and weight copying**: stakers and validators can delegate to, or follow, the einstein validator's
  weights through Bittensor's child-hotkey mechanism while independent validation matures.
- **An open job queue**: opening client jobs to other staked validators, each routing, gating and scoring its share
  with the hosted Watt check.
- **Mainnet**: registration on Bittensor mainnet (netuid to be announced).
```
